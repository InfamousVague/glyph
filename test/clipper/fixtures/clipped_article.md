# The Quiet Garden

For the spring planting.

---

Most of the work in a *winter* garden happens where nobody can see it. The soil keeps going long after the last leaves have dropped, and a gardener who leaves it covered gets **twice the harvest** in spring. This is a short account of how a small city plot, about the size of two parking spaces, was kept alive through a hard season without heat, without a greenhouse and with very little money.

We measured the beds every week with a cheap probe, wrote the numbers in a notebook, and read them back at the end of the season. The probe cost $5 and the notebook $10, which is the whole budget. Our `soil_temp` log is linked [here](https://field.example/data/soil.csv), and the questions we still have are marked like \{?this one}.

## What we covered the beds with

Three things worked, and one did not:

- Straw, laid thick, then wetted so it would not blow away.
- Leaves from the street trees, shredded first:
  - oak takes longest to break down,
  - plane and lime are gone by March.
- Cardboard under compost, for the beds that would be dug first.

Plastic sheeting did not work. It kept the rain off, and the worms left.

### The order we did it in

1. Clear the last of the summer crop.
2. Water the beds well.
3. Lay the cover, thickest on the north side.

> Feed the soil, not the plant. The plant will take care of itself.
