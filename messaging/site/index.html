One inbox

Telegram and Signal, right beside your texts.

Sign in once and your Telegram and Signal chats join your texts in one list, newest first, each marked with its network. Read them, answer them and send pictures without switching apps.

Telegram

Android · Mac
  • Your private chats and groups, with their pictures, in the same list as your texts
  • Photos, videos, voice messages and files, both ways
  • Swipe to archive or delete, done on Telegram itself
  • Telegram's sign-in codes land in your Codes tab, and the spam filter checks strangers' chats

How you connect: sign in with your Telegram number, the code Telegram sends you and your two-step password if you have one. It runs on Telegram's own client library, TDLib.

Signal

Android · Mac with Apple silicon
  • Messages and groups, with replies and reactions
  • Pictures, read receipts and deletes, both ways
  • Your Signal contacts' names and faces
  • Still end-to-end encrypted, with Signal's own protocol

How you connect: Uplink links as another device, as Signal Desktop does: in Signal on your phone, open Settings › Linked devices and scan the code. Signal gives a new device no history, so you'll see messages from then on.

Texts and picture messages Uplink chats RCS, through Google Messages Telegram Signal Bluesky · soon Mastodon · soon

Sign in to your chat networks once in your browser with the Chrome extension (with the downloads above), and every device on your account is signed in too. Not in the Chrome Web Store yet: unzip it and add it in chrome://extensions with Load unpacked. How to add it, and what it can see.

Each network runs on your own device and talks straight to its own servers. Your chats, codes and sign-ins never pass through ours. The Signal helper is built on the open-source mautrix-signal and Signal's libsignal, and its source is published with every release (AGPL). Telegram and Signal aren't on Windows yet, and their calls aren't included: messages only.

On your computer

Everything, on the big screen.

Uplink for Mac, Windows and Linux shows every conversation, sends texts through your phone (sealed between the two), and joins calls and spaces itself.

  1. Download for Mac, Windows or Linux. On a Mac, open the disk image and drag the app to Applications. On Windows, run the installer (Windows asks once, as it does for any new app). On Ubuntu, Debian and their family, open the .deb to install it; on any other Linux, unpack the .tar.gz and run ghost-messaging.
  2. Pair with your phone. On your phone open Settings › Computers and scan the code the computer shows.
  3. That's it. Both keep themselves up to date.
Private by default

Nothing goes where it shouldn't.

No account to make. What travels through our server between friends is sealed on your device first, so the server passes it along and can't read it.

Sealed end to end

Chats between Uplink users, your computer's link to your phone, and calls are encrypted on the devices themselves.

No account

Your number stays on your phone. A username and linked accounts are yours to turn on, and to remove.

Texts stay texts

SMS and picture messages go through your carrier as they always have. Nothing is copied to us.
