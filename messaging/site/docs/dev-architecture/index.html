How the app is built

The major parts of Ghost Messaging, where each lives in the source, and what it talks to.

Ghost Messaging is one Flutter app for Android, iPhone, Mac, Windows and Linux, a small relay server, and a few helper programs. This page is a map of the source for someone about to read or change it: what each part is for, where it lives, and what it talks to. Paths are from the repository's root (github.com/InfamousVague/ghost-messaging).

Where things are

FolderWhat it holds

android/The Android half, in Kotlin: SMS and MMS, notifications, encryption keys, the link to paired computers, the Google Messages bridge, the updater.
ios/, macos/, windows/, linux/Each platform's runner, with the native pieces the app calls: call recording, captions, transcripts, sounds, updates, deep links.

test/, integration_test/The app's tests, and the pictures its screen tests are held to (test/shots).

One app, several ways of running

Every screen asks for what it needs through one object, Sms.instance (lib/sms.dart): the conversations, a thread's messages, sending, the settings. Sms passes each call by name to whatever is set as Sms.calls, and which object that is decides how the app runs. The screens are the same in every case.

Answered byWhereUsed when
The platform channel ghost_messaging/smslib/sms.dart, android/…/MainActivity.ktAn Android phone. Kotlin reads and writes Android's own SMS and MMS database.
OverlayCallslib/standalone/overlay.dartAndroid, wrapped round the channel: conversations with @username friends are answered in Dart, the rest passed on.
StandaloneCallslib/standalone/standalone.dartAn iPhone, or a computer with no phone linked: the app's own network and nothing from a phone.
LinkCallslib/desktop/remote.dartA computer paired with an Android phone: each call becomes a sealed request to the phone.
DeskOwnCallslib/desktop/own_calls.dartA paired computer the phone has handed its identity to: friends and circles run on the computer itself, texts still come from the phone.

lib/main.dart chooses: a computer goes to desktopMain() (lib/desktop/main.dart), an iPhone starts on its own, and Android starts on the channel. From there both paths load the person's choices (AppState, lib/app_state.dart), the plugins, the API, and then the network through startPeople (lib/people.dart), which starts the routes, friends, groups, spaces and circles in that order.

Texts on Android

The app keeps no copy of your texts: they live in Android's SMS and MMS database, which is why changing texting apps loses nothing. The Kotlin side is in android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/infamousvague/ghostmessaging.

FileWhat it does
MainActivity.ktAnswers the channel's calls and sends events back (ghost_messaging/events): a conversation changed, something to open.
Store.ktReads and writes Android's database: the list, threads, messages, contacts.
Messaging.ktSending (Sender) and the receivers for texts arriving, MMS arriving, and sent and delivered reports.
Pdu.ktThe MMS wire format, written and read by hand.
Notifier.ktNotifications, with Reply and Mark as read.
E2e.kt, E2eCrypto.ktEncrypted texts with one person, and the phone's key pair, held under the Android Keystore.
GmBridge.kt, GmWeb.ktRunning beside Google Messages: reading its notifications, or its web connection through sidecar/gmessages.
Desk.kt, DeskSession.kt, DeskCrypto.kt, DeskService.ktThe phone's half of the link to paired computers.

Routes: how apps reach each other

Everything between two Ghost apps (a friend's message, a call's setup, a space's chat) goes through lib/net. Its idea is the route: one way of getting bytes to another install, behind an interface (Carrier, lib/net/carrier.dart) so a new one can be added without the app above knowing.

PartWhereWhat it does
Envelope and packetlib/net/packet.dartAn envelope is a small JSON message with a kind (text, call, room, hello…). A packet is an envelope as a route carries it: a version byte, the sender's 16-byte name, then the envelope, sealed. Long ones cross a short route in parts.
The switchboardlib/net/switchboard.dartChooses a route for each thing sent, falls back to the next, seals and opens, joins parts, drops second copies, and answers what asks for an answer.
The hub routelib/net/hub_carrier.dartA WebSocket to the relay server's hub. Also where presence and rooms come from. Its protocol.
The SMS routelib/net/sms_carrier.dart, android/…/NetSms.ktPackets as data texts: 133 bytes each, six at most, costing a text each, and able to wake a closed app.
Keys and sealinglib/net/crypto.dart, android/…/Net.ktP-256 key agreement, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM. Kotlin and Dart are held to the same bytes by tests on both sides.
The app's hold on it alllib/net/net.dartNet: starts the routes, knows who can be reached, and is what the rest of the app sends through.

The routes are tried in the person's order (Settings › Routes). One is passed over when it is off, down, can't reach that person, can't hold the packet, or costs money and the message isn't one allowed to. Only the hub and SMS routes exist today; the interface leaves room for a local-network and a peer-to-peer one.

Who someone is

  • An install is a P-256 key pair. Its name everywhere on the network is the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of its public key, in hex. The relay knows installs by that name and nothing else.
  • An account (lib/account) is twelve recovery words. From them come an address that names the account, and the keys that seal its backup, which is kept on the relay sealed twice: once under the password, once under the words. How your account works is the plain account of it.
  • A profile (lib/me/me.dart): the username, linked handles and verified number, kept by the relay's directory as hashes.
  • Friends: by number (lib/friends/friends.dart, for people encryption is on with) and by username (lib/friends/buddies.dart). Groups of friends are lib/friends/groups.dart: each message goes to each member by the routes.

Circles, spaces and calls

A space (in the code, a hangout or voice channel) is 32 random bytes, its seed. The room's name on the hub, the token that lets someone in and the key everything in it is sealed with are all made from the seed (VoiceChannel, lib/voice/voice.dart), so whoever holds a space's link holds all three and the relay holds none.

  • lib/voice/channels.dart (Voice): the spaces someone is in, and making, sharing, joining and leaving them.
  • lib/voice/hangout.dart (HangoutRoom): a space's chat. History is passed between members; the hub only holds packets for whoever is away.
  • lib/voice/rtc.dart: the call itself, WebRTC from each member to each other. Offers, answers and candidates are sealed with the space's key and said in its room; sound and picture never go through the relay's hub.
  • lib/voice/roles.dart: a space's roles, as a numbered list signed by its owner.
  • lib/circles: a circle is one seed and a signed manifest listing its spaces, roles and settings (CircleManifest, lib/circles/circle.dart). Members pass the newest manifest to each other.
  • lib/files: a Files space. Files are sealed on the device in 256 KiB pieces (lib/net/file_seal.dart) and kept by the relay, which sees sizes and never names or contents.

Other networks

lib/inbox puts other services' chats in the same list. Each is an InboxNetwork (lib/inbox/inbox.dart) with its own way of connecting:

NetworkHow
TelegramTDLib, loaded into the app (lib/inbox/td_json.dart).
Signal, WhatsApp, MatrixA helper program each (sidecar/), started by the app and spoken to in lines of JSON over its input and output. Each helper's PROTOCOL.md says what is said.
Google Chat, Microsoft TeamsTheir own web APIs, straight from the app.

Nothing from these networks goes through Ghost's relay.

Paired computers

A computer paired with a phone shows the phone's texts by asking the phone for them. The computer's half is lib/desktop/link (PhoneLink, LinkSession); the phone's is DeskSession.kt. They connect over the local network where they can and through the relay's pairing channel where they can't, and either way every frame is sealed with keys made from the pairing, so the relay passes bytes it can't read. What the computer has read is kept sealed on its disk (lib/desktop/cache.dart).

Plugins and the Marketplace

A plugin adds to the conversations list and to texts through a few fixed points (lib/plugins/plugin.dart): tagging a message, a section or tab in the list, a button, a rule for what arrives. The built-in ones are Dart classes (lib/plugins/registry.dart). One from another developer is data, never code: a JSON file (lib/plugins/pack) the app reads into those same points, held to what it declared by GatedHost.

The Marketplace (lib/market, and relay/lib/market.dart on the server) lists reviewed plugins. An approval is a reviewer's signature over the plugin's id, version and its file's SHA-256, checked against keys the app ships with, at install and at every start: the server can withhold a plugin but can't make one look reviewed.

The relay server

relay/bin/relay.dart starts one HTTP server; relay/lib/relay.dart sends each request to its part.

PartFileWhat it does
Pairing channelsrelay.dartPasses sealed frames between a phone and its computer while both are connected. Keeps no frame.
The hubhub.dartWhere installs find each other: signing in with a key, packets passed or held for whoever is away, presence, rooms.
The directoryhandles.dart, accounts.dart, invites.dart, invite_words.dartUsernames, linked handles, number verification, sealed account backups, invitations and circles' invite words. Reached through the hub's connection.
Filesfiles.dart, files_roster.dartFiles spaces' sealed pieces and their log.
Marketplacemarket.dart, market_pack.dart, market_store.dartPlugins' submissions, review and listing.
Pushes, feedbackpushes.dart, feedback.dartA "New message" push to an iPhone something is held for; feedback sent from the app.
Arithmeticp256.dart, secp256k1.dart, sha256.dart, keccak.dartSignature checking and hashes, written out so the relay depends on nothing.

What it can see is the same throughout: which installs talk, when, and how much. Its HTTP routes and its WebSocket protocols are documented in full.

Screens

  • lib/ui/shell.dart (AppShell) is the frame: the list and what is open beside it, side by side where the window is wide.
  • lib/ui/conversations.dart is the list, lib/ui/thread.dart a conversation, lib/ui/hangout_screen.dart a space, lib/ui/voice_screen.dart a call.
  • lib/ui/settings.dart and settings_panes.dart are Settings, built from the rows in lib/kit.
  • lib/desktop/ui is what only a computer has: the set-up wizard, the title bar, the update bar.
  • lib/art holds the icons, lib/looks.dart the looks, and lib/effects.dart, stickers.dart and reactions.dart what texts can wear.

The app's own API

lib/api is a registry of methods (conversations.list, messages.send…), each with its parameters, what it answers and an example. The same methods are called from Settings' own test area, over HTTP on the device, and from anywhere through the relay, sealed. See API overview and Postman.
