Circles, spaces and calls

Calls

Joining voice, the call's controls, each person's volume, and guests from a browser.

A chat-and-voice space has a call that's always open, like a voice channel: hop in, talk, hop out. Sound goes straight between the people in it, sealed end to end, at high quality.

Join a call

Open a chat-and-voice space and tap Join voice. Who's already in it shows above. On a phone, a space with people talking opens straight on its call.

In the call

ControlDoes
MuteStops your microphone.
DeafenHear nobody, and say nothing. Hear again undoes it.
CameraTurns your video on or off.
Share screenShows everyone your screen, or one window. See Share your screen.
LeaveLeaves the call. The space and its chat stay.

On a computer, a circle's space shows the call above its chat. Chat, beside Leave, hides the chat so the call has the whole pane; press it again to bring the chat back.

While you're in a call and look at something else, a small bubble of faces floats on top; tap it to go back.

Share your screen

Press Share screen in the call. In some call designs it's in the call's menu instead. It works on Mac, Windows, Linux and Android.

  • On a computer, pick a whole screen or one window. On a Mac, the first time, allow Ghost Messaging in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen Recording, then try again.
  • On Android, the phone asks before it shares, and you can choose one app or the whole screen. A notification says Sharing your screen while it's on.

Everyone else sees your screen large and whole, never cropped. Back to call puts it away, and a line over the call says who is sharing, with View to bring it back. While you share, a line says You're sharing your screen, with Stop.

Your screen goes out in place of your camera: while you share, others see your screen, not your camera, and your camera is back when you stop. Sound from your computer isn't shared yet. If your role in a space can't show video, it can't share a screen either. Like the rest of the call, it goes encrypted between you: the relay can't see it.

Each person's volume

Press and hold someone in the call:

There are two mutes, and they are separate. Mute for me is yours alone: only you stop hearing them, they aren't told, and nobody else is affected. Their row shows a crossed speaker and muted for you.

In a space, a moderator also sees Mute for everyone (their role needs Mute others, and has to be above the other person's). Nobody in the space hears that person, and they can't unmute themselves: their microphone button shows a lock, and says why when they press it. Everyone sees a lock and muted for everyone on their row, so it is clear it wasn't their own doing. Any moderator above them can switch it off again. It doesn't rely on the muted person's app: every app in the call silences them itself.

A moderator's mute lasts while the person stays in the space. An admin's or the owner's is written into the space's roles, so it still holds when they come back. A moderator can also put someone out of the space if their role allows it.

A stage: a few speak, many listen

For a talk, a show or a town hall. Whoever manages the space opens the call's menu and chooses Make it a stage. From then on, everyone who comes in listens: they hear the speakers and can write in the chat, but their microphone stays off.

  • Raise your hand (a listener's menu) puts a ✋ by your name, so the hosts know you'd like to speak.
  • A host presses and holds someone and chooses Bring up to speak, or Move to the audience to send them back.
  • The menu says how many are listening and how many hands are up. End the stage lets everyone speak again.

Listeners don't connect to each other, only to the speakers, so a stage holds far more people than an ordinary call.

Sound settings

Settings › Voice and video has your microphone and speaker, the sound's quality, stereo, music mode (for playing an instrument), and noise removal (RNNoise or DeepFilterNet), which cleans background noise off your voice before it's sent. The call's menu has Voice settings… too.

Guests from a browser

Someone without the app can join a space's call from a browser. Copy the space's link from its menu (Invite people) and send it. They open it, choose their microphone and camera, and tap Join as a guest, or sign up with a username kept in that browser.

Strict networks

Calls connect through Ghost's relay first, which gets through almost any network: a phone on a mobile network, a workplace, school or hotel network. If the relay can't be reached, the call tries a direct path between you instead, by itself. The relay passes the call along still encrypted between you: it can't hear or see it.

With plugins

Add Record call, Live captions, Watch together, Soundboard or Auto leveler for more in calls. They show in the call's menu, and Record call and Soundboard put a button in the space's header.
