A Ghost account keeps who you are: your username, friends and circles. With it you can sign in on another device and be the same you there, or get everything back on a new phone. Your texts aren't part of it; they stay in your phone's own store.

Create an account

You make one the first time you open Ghost: pick a username and a password and tap Continue. Your account is made then and there.

The username is how people find you. The password is at least 10 characters; with it and your username you sign in on another device. It's stretched on your device first, so this takes a moment.

Continue

Ghost shows 12 words. Write them down, on paper, in this order, and keep them somewhere safe. Make sure nobody can see your screen.

I've written them down

Anyone with your 12 words can sign in as you, and without them (or your password) nobody, not even us, can get your account back. Ghost Messaging will never ask for them except to sign in.

Tap them in order, or type three of them. See the words again takes you back. Skip this check goes straight on; you can see your words again any time with Settings › Me › Account › Show recovery words.

Set Ghost up before sign-up asked for a password, and have no account yet? Make one in Settings › Me › Account › Create an account: a password, then the same words.

Sign in on another device

On the new device: Settings › Me › Account › Sign in to your Ghost account. Sign in with your username and password, or with your 12 recovery words alone.

Sign in

Signing in makes the device your account. Anything on it from before, as someone else, is replaced, and the app starts again. Your texts are not touched.

Devices, password and signing out

Everything is in Settings › Me › Account:

  • Change password: your backup is sealed again with the new one. Your recovery words stay the same.
  • Signed in on these devices: remove a device you no longer have. The next time it connects it's signed out: an iPhone, a computer without a phone or a browser deletes everything of your account from itself; an Android phone, or a computer linked to one, forgets your recovery words and its keys for circles and forward secrecy, and keeps its username, friends and conversations. Each browser you sign in on, and each private window, is a device here until it signs out or you remove it.
  • Sign out of this device: the device becomes someone new. Your friends and circles stay with your account and come back when you sign in again. On an iPhone or a computer without a phone, your conversations, spaces and choices stay on the device unless you choose Sign out and delete everything here; on an Android phone your texts and conversations stay.
  • Sign out of this browser: in a browser, everything of your account is deleted from it, always.

A browser keeps neither your recovery words nor your password's key, so it asks for your password to show the words, change the password, list or remove your devices, and bring your backup up to date (Update your backup). Everything else it keeps is sealed with a key the browser holds for the page, but anything running in the page, such as a browser extension allowed on it, could open it. On a computer you share, sign out when you're done.
