Messenger and Instagram have no way in for other apps, so Ghost signs in to them with your browser's own sign-in. The Ghost Messaging keys extension does that part for you: sign in to Messenger or Instagram as you always do, in Chrome, and every device signed in to your Ghost account is signed in too. No copying anything from one device to the next.

Download the extension (for Chrome and browsers built on it; version 0.1.1, 43 KB)

Add it to Chrome

It isn't in the Chrome Web Store yet, so Chrome adds it from the folder you download. Edge, Brave and other browsers built on Chrome take it the same way.

Download ghost-messaging-keys.zip above and unzip it. Keep the folder somewhere it'll stay (not your Downloads, if you clear that): Chrome loads the extension from it each time it starts.

Go to chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode, at the top right.

Click Load unpacked and choose the folder you unzipped. Ghost Messaging keys appears in the list; pin it from the puzzle piece in the toolbar so it's at hand.

Click the extension and sign in with your username and password, or your recovery words, as you would on a new device. That's what lets it seal your sign-ins so only your devices can open them.

Using it

Sign in to Messenger (messenger.com or facebook.com) or Instagram in that browser. A few seconds later the extension seals that sign-in and hands it to Ghost's relay. Each of your devices picks it up, switches on that plugin (Settings › Connections › Plugins) and signs in. When the sites refresh your sign-in, it's sent again by itself.

To stop, sign out of the extension or remove it from Chrome. Your devices keep the sign-ins they already have until you sign out of those plugins in Ghost.

What it can and can't see

  • It reads only the sign-in cookies of Messenger and Instagram: nothing from other sites, and nothing from the pages themselves.
  • What it sends is sealed with a key made from your account's recovery words. Ghost's relay keeps one sealed copy for your account and can't open it.
  • Your password and recovery words are used the moment you type them and never kept. Unless you tick Stay signed in, the extension forgets your account when the browser closes.
  • These are your own sign-ins to those services, used the way their websites use them. They don't allow other apps officially, and may sign you out or limit your account.
