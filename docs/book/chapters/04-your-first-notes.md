# Your first notes

This chapter covers making a note, naming it, finding it again, and putting it away. There's no Save button anywhere: a note is kept as you type.

## Make a note

- **In Advanced,** tap **+** on the dock (its label is **Write a note**). The **New** sheet opens. Choose **Note**.
- **In Simple,** tap the round **+** at the bottom. A blank note opens straight away. Press and hold the **+** for the New sheet.
- **On a Mac,** press ⌘N.

A blank page opens with the caret on its first line. Start typing.

The New sheet also offers:

| Row | What it makes |
|---|---|
| **Note** | A page of Markdown, typed or said |
| **Today** | Today's note, opened, or made the first time |
| **Entry in** a journal | A new entry in the journal you wrote in last, after asking what it starts with |
| **Canvas** | Cards on a page with lines between them |
| **Notebook** | Notes in an order with an index, or a journal of dated entries |
| **Meeting** | A meeting recording, on Android and the Mac. The screen can go off while it records. |
| **From a shared link** | A copy of a note or notebook someone shared with you |

Where this device can record its own sound in a meeting, a switch for that sits under **Meeting**.

In Simple, everything after Note is behind **More…**, which opens in place.

## The first line is the name

A note's first line is its name. It's what you see in your lists, and it names the note's file too. Put `# ` in front of it to make it a title:

```
# Weekend trip
```

There's no separate title field. Change the first line and the note's name changes with it. Two notes can have the same name, but a link by that name opens only one of them, so a name of its own is better.

## Names and templates on a blank page

While a new note is still blank, a few things wait under its first line.

**Names.** Four ready-made names based on today, always in this order: the date in words (`Monday 26 October 2026`), the date as `2026-10-26`, the date and time (`2026-10-26 14.05`), and the week as `2026-W44`. Tap one and it becomes the note's title. A name another note already has, even one in the Trash, isn't offered.

**Templates,** under **Start from a template**:

| Template | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **A day** | A note for today. If today already has one, the card says so and makes a second. |
| **A meeting** | Named for this minute, with who was there, notes and to-dos |
| **A checklist** | A name to type, then a to-do |
| **Notes on a book** | Its title, who wrote it, notes and quotes |
| **A map at the top** | Where you are, drawn large above the words. On Android only. |
| **A page to read** | A large title, a lead line and a column set for reading |
| **Bug report** | A ticket: how to make it happen, what should, and what did |
| **Feature** | A ticket: the problem, the proposal, and when it's done |

Tap one and the blank page becomes that note, with the caret where you write first. Changed your mind? Undo takes it back to a blank page.

**Your templates** opens a notebook called Templates, made the first time, with the built-in ones as its pages. Change them, remove them, or add your own, and from then on a blank page offers yours, in that notebook's order.

The names and templates go away at your first letter. A note you leave without writing anything is never kept.

## Speak a note

On Android and the Mac, you can say a note instead. Tap the microphone (**Speak a voice note** on the dock, or the round microphone in Simple), talk, and tap **Done**. [[Speaking your notes]] has the whole story.

## Two ways to see a note

A note can show its marks, or hide them:

- **Markdown** shows the words formatted, with the marks still on the page, dimmed.
- **Formatted** hides the marks. The line you're editing shows its marks again so you can change them.

**In Advanced,** the round button just before the three dots switches between them. Its picture is the view you're in. **In Simple,** the switch is a row in the three dots' sheet: **Show the marks** or **Show the formatted note**. Simple starts in Formatted; Advanced starts in Markdown. Your choice holds for every note. See [[Markdown and the Ghost.md marks]].

## Pin, archive and delete

Open a note's **More** sheet with the three dots at the top (**More for this note**):

- **Pin to the top** keeps the note at the top of your home page. **Unpin** takes it off.
- **Archive** takes it off the home page and out of your lists, without deleting it.
- **Move to Trash** takes it out of every list, with **Undo** for a few seconds.

In Advanced, Pin and Archive also come out of the sheet onto the note's own bar when there's room.

**From your list of notes:**

- **Swipe a note right** to pin or unpin it.
- **Swipe left** to archive it, or further left to delete it. In Simple, swiping left deletes.
- **On a Mac,** right-click a note for **Open**, **Add a comment**, **Pin** or **Unpin**, **Archive**, **Move to** a folder, and **Delete**.

A deleted note always goes to the Trash first, with Undo.

## The Trash

Open the Trash from the last row of the sidebar, from **All notes**, or by typing Trash in search and commands. Each note says when it went in and how long it has left.

- **Restore** puts a note back where it was, folder, workspace and pin included.
- **Delete for good** deletes one note. It asks once first.
- **Empty Trash** deletes them all. It asks once too.

Notes in the Trash are deleted for good thirty days after they went in. An organization's notes are the exception: they wait until you empty the Trash yourself.

## Find a note again

- **Search on the home page.** The search at the top narrows the page as you type, every word anywhere in a note. Archived notes stay out unless you ask for them: see [[Workspaces, folders and search]].
- **Search and commands.** In Advanced, the dock's **Search and commands** opens one list of your notes by name and everything the app can do. Start typing. On a Mac, ⌘K opens it too.
- **The home page.** In Advanced, the home page starts with your pinned notes, then the four you touched last as cards, then everything else by when: Today, Yesterday and so on. Other layouts are in Settings: see [[Home, tabs and the sidebar]]. In Simple, notes come in groups, **Pinned** first. **All notes** at the foot opens every one.
- **Links.** Put a note's name in double square brackets to link to it. See [[Links, tags and footnotes]].

A note opens where you left it. Pinch with two fingers to make a note's words larger or smaller; every note then opens at the size you pinched to.

## Find and replace

**Find and replace** is in the More sheet. Or press and hold some words and choose **Find**. On a Mac, ⌘F.

## Where you wrote it

On Android, each new note you make keeps where it was written, in the note's own front matter, with a small map at the top. You can switch this off: **Tag new notes with my location**, in Settings › Account, under **Location**. The switch is on at first, and stays on the device you set it on.

- **Add my location** and **Remove location**, in a note's More sheet, add or take away one note's place by hand.
- **Map on a tagged note** and **Place names**, beside the switch, choose whether the map is drawn and whether the place gets a name.
- With **Local only** on, no location is looked up.

A Mac and an iPhone can't say where they are yet. A note tagged on your phone shows its place on them too.

A link you share leaves the place out unless you say otherwise on the note.

## A header image

With the Lovart plugin added, a note can have a picture across its top, made for it. **Header image** in a note's More sheet makes one now. With **Header images for notes** on, in Settings › Lovart, one is made by itself when you leave a note of 25 words or more. See [[Lovart]].

## Learn as you go

- **How to format a note** is the tutorial note every new library starts with.
- **Ghost.md Academy** teaches one mark at a time, in short lessons. Open it from Settings › About. If you know Markdown already, switch on **I know Markdown already** to learn only Ghost.md's own marks.
- **The cheat sheet** puts every mark on one page: Settings › About › **Cheat sheet**, or **Formatting cheat sheet** in a note's More sheet.
