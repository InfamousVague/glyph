Ghost.md

Privacy

Last updated 5 November 2026

Ghost.md is a notes app made by Matt Wisniewski ("we", "us"). This page says what happens to your notes and your data when you use the Ghost.md apps for Android, iPhone and Mac, the web version, and the ghostmarkdown.com site.

The short version
  • Your notes are files on your device. You don't need an account to use Ghost.md.
  • In the apps, what you say is turned into text on your device. Your voice isn't sent anywhere to be transcribed.
  • If you make an account to sync, everything is encrypted on your device before it's sent, so we can't read it.
  • If you join a team (an organization), its name and who is in it are kept in the clear, so teammates can be invited by handle. Notes you file in it are shared with its members, encrypted so that only they can read them. Your other notes stay yours alone.
  • The AI assistant and the plugins are optional. They work only with an account or key you set up yourself, and they send your notes only to the service you chose, from your device.
  • No ads and no tracking, and we never sell your data or share it beyond the services you choose to use. The apps send anonymous usage statistics (which features are used, never what you write), and you can turn them off in Settings › Privacy.
  • You can delete your account, and everything it keeps on our servers, from the app at any time.

On your device

Your notes, their recordings and their pictures are stored on your device, as plain Markdown files and the files beside them. Voice transcription and note formatting run on your device, using models the app downloads once. We can't see anything that stays on your device.

Your device's own backup (for example Android's backup to your Google account) may include the app's files. That backup is between you and your device's maker, under their terms.

Anonymous usage statistics

The Android, iPhone and Mac apps send anonymous usage statistics to help us see which features are used and what goes wrong. They go to PostHog, an analytics service, whose servers for our project are in the United States. Each one is an event, such as "app opened", "note created", "plugin switched on", "background job failed" or "screen viewed", with a few details from fixed lists: the app's version, the kind of device (phone, Fold, tablet or computer) and its operating system, the language your device is set to (for example "en", never the region), whether the app is in Simple or Advanced mode, which plugins are on, and buckets such as how long something took ("5-30s") or how many ("6-20"). An uncaught error is counted by the name of its kind (for example "FormatException"), never its message.

They are tied only to a random ID that the app makes on your device the first time it sends them. That ID is never linked to your account, your handle, an email address or an organization, is never sent to our own service, and does not sync between your devices. PostHog is set not to build a profile of you, not to record your screen, and to discard your IP address; the app also asks it not to look up where you are.

They never contain your content: not your notes, their titles or words, file or folder names, what you search for, prompts you write, links, names, handles, email addresses, organization names, or ids from Notion, GitHub, Figma or any other service.

You can turn them off in Settings › Privacy › Anonymous usage stats, or on the privacy page of the walkthrough when you first open the app. Turning them off deletes the random ID and anything not yet sent, and nothing more is sent; turning them on again makes a new ID. With Local only on, nothing is sent either. The web version sends none.

Your account, if you make one

An account is optional. It keeps your notes the same on each of your devices. When you have one, our sync service stores:

  • Your handle (the name you sign in with), and when the account was created and last signed in to.
  • A value made from your password, so you can sign in. Your password itself never leaves your device: the app derives two keys from it, and sends only one, which we store as a salted hash. The other, which unlocks your notes, stays on your devices.
  • Your synced notes, recordings, pictures and settings, encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) with a key only your devices and your recovery codes can open. We can't read them.
  • What's needed to sync, which we can see: random ids for each note and file, when each was changed, their sizes and how many there are, a picture's file type, and which kind of device each of your devices is (for example "Mac").
  • A public key for each of your devices, so a signed-in device can stay signed in.

We don't store your IP address with your account. Our service uses it briefly, in memory, to limit repeated sign-in attempts. Our web server keeps ordinary access logs (IP address, time and the address requested) for security and troubleshooting, and we don't use them for anything else.

Organizations and notifications (optional)

You can make an organization, a team, and invite other Ghost.md accounts to it by handle. So that an invitation can reach another account, an organization is not encrypted the way your notes are. For each organization you make or join, our service stores and can see: its name and colour, who owns it, when it was made, and who is in it by handle, each with their role (owner, admin or member), whether they have joined or are invited, who invited them and when. If someone declines an invitation, that is kept for a day so the organization can't ask them again at once.

Inviting someone tells you whether a handle exists ("No one has that handle."). That is the one place a signed-in account can learn this, and it is limited to thirty invitations an hour per account and per network address.

An owner or admin can also invite someone by email. We then store the email address, who sent the invitation, when it was sent and when it stops, and send one email through our email provider, Mailgun, with a link to join. We keep the address only while the invitation can be used: it is deleted when the invitation is accepted, when it is turned off, or within an hour of it expiring after 14 days. Accounts have no email address, so an invitation never says whether the address belongs to a Ghost.md account. Sending is limited per account, per network address, per organization and per address invited.

Your account also has a list of notifications. For those the service writes itself (an invitation, someone joining or leaving a team, a rename), it can see the kind, when, who caused it, which organization, and the few words in it, such as the organization's name. For those your own devices or a connected AI assistant write about your own notes (a note the assistant created or edited, a meeting written up, a note kept twice by a sync), the contents are encrypted on the device like a note, and the service sees only the kind and the time. For every notification, the service can see when you read it and whether you hid it, so that follows you across your devices. Which notifications you see is a setting in Settings › Notifications; an invitation is always shown.

Notes filed in an organization are shared with its members. A note you file in an organization's workspace, or make there, is the team's: every member can read it, edit it, comment on it and delete it, and members who have it open at the same time see each other's typing, cursor and colour. Notes you don't file there stay yours alone. Team notes are encrypted on a member's device with a key made there and held only by the organization's members, wrapped separately for each of them, so our service stores them and cannot read them: not their titles, words, comments, pictures or version history, and not who wrote a comment.

What our service can see about a team note: which organization it belongs to, its id, its size, when it changed, and which account wrote each change. It also keeps each member's public encryption key and chosen colour. While team notes are being edited live, the service that passes the edits along can see which signed-in account has which team note open, and when; it can also see that a member's device is in the app and when it has just saved a change to the organization. The edits themselves, the note's title and where a cursor is are encrypted, and nothing passed along is stored. Every change to a team note is listed by handle in the note's version history, which is inside the encryption and shown to its members; the organization's activity log shows it to them alongside the membership events described above.

You can decline an invitation, leave an organization at any time, and mute its notifications; an owner or admin can remove a member. Someone who leaves or is removed loses access to the service at once. The next time a remaining member's device syncs, the team's notes are re-encrypted under a new key they don't have. They keep whatever their devices had already downloaded. The organization's key is also kept, wrapped, for someone who has been invited and has not yet accepted, so it is ready when they do; they cannot read it or anything else until they join. Deleting an organization removes its notes, files and keys from our service. Because we cannot read team notes, we do not review them. You can report a member, or something written in an organization, from its settings under Report: that opens an email to the address under Contact, and since we cannot read the note ourselves, the email has to carry the words it is about. An owner or an admin can remove a member, and anyone can leave. Share only with people you trust.

Shared links

When you share a note or a notebook, the app encrypts a copy and uploads it. The key to open it is in the link itself, after the "#", which browsers don't send to servers, so we can't read what you shared. Anyone you give the link to can read it. You can stop a link at any time in Settings › Account, and it stops opening at once. Links are deleted with your account.

Live typing (optional)

If you turn on live typing, what you type in a note open on two of your devices is passed between them through our service as it's typed. It's encrypted the same way as your notes, and our service doesn't store it.

The AI assistant in the app (optional)

Ghost.md has an AI assistant that answers questions about your notes and writes into them. It works only once you choose an AI provider in Settings › AI and give it your own key from that provider: Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI, Google (Gemini), Aster, or an address of your own (for example OpenRouter, or a model running on your own computer). The provider bills your key for what you ask. Your key is kept in your device's secure storage. If you sync, it follows you to your other devices sealed inside your encrypted settings, so we can't read it.

When you ask the assistant something, your device sends the request straight to the provider you chose, never through our servers: what you asked, the conversation so far, and the notes the assistant reads to answer. Once you have set a provider up, a few features also send to it without asking each time: meeting write-ups (unless you choose On this device under Settings › AI › Meeting summaries), the to-dos you write in your Ask Claude note, and short recordings from a watch. Cleanup, Wrapped, the contradiction finder and Note decay's AI checks do the same once you add them, and Wrapped asks before its first pass. Several of these can use an AI model that runs on your device instead, which sends nothing. What a provider receives is covered by its own privacy policy and terms.

Pictures in the AI chat: you can give the assistant pictures, for example a photo of a list of appointments to turn into to-dos. Your device makes each picture smaller and sends it with your message to the provider you chose, and only if its model can read pictures. The copy the picture chooser makes is deleted as soon as it is read. A picture is kept only in the app's memory for that conversation, unless you turn on "Keep pictures in notes", which lets the assistant put it in a note.

Connecting an AI assistant from outside the app (optional)

You can also connect an AI assistant, such as Claude, to your notes. If you connect it through our hosted connection, you sign in on a page that gives our server your notes' key for the length of that connection, so it can read and write your notes when the assistant asks. The key is held in memory while it is used. So that a restart of our server doesn't disconnect you, it is also kept on disk, but only sealed: it opens only with a token the assistant holds, and we don't keep those tokens. It is forgotten when you disconnect, when you sign out everywhere, or after 7 days without use. What the assistant reads goes to its maker (for example Anthropic), under their privacy policy. Notes the assistant writes are marked with its name.

Plugins (optional)

Plugins add features that work with other services. Most are off until you switch them on in Settings › Plugins or the Market, and the few that are on from the start, such as Notion and GitHub, send nothing until you connect them. Each uses your own account, key or address, kept in your device's secure storage (and, if you sync, sealed inside your encrypted settings). Unless it says otherwise below, your device talks to the service directly, not through our servers. Local only holds every plugin that uses the network off.

  • Header images and pictures (Lovart): with your own Lovart keys, Lovart draws pictures for your notes, using your Lovart credits. While "Header images for notes" is on (it is, once Lovart is set up), leaving a note of 25 words or more sends its title, its first 1,500 characters and some of its headings to Lovart to draw a banner. A picture you ask for in a note sends only its description. Switch header images off on the plugin's page.
  • Voice-overs (ElevenLabs): with your own ElevenLabs key, a voice-over you ask for sends the readable words of that note to ElevenLabs, which sends back the audio.
  • Notion: if you connect Notion, your device talks to Notion directly, sending the tasks you choose to send, and an import reads the pages you shared with Ghost.md, with their pictures and files. Signing in to Notion passes through our server, which holds the sign-in tokens in memory for up to ten minutes until your device collects them. The tokens are then kept on your device.
  • GitHub: if you connect GitHub, your device talks to GitHub directly with the token you give it, which is kept on your device. It sends the to-dos you turn into issues, as their titles, and reads the repositories your notes link to.
  • Back up to git: commits your Library to a git repository you choose (on GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg or any git host) and pushes it there. The backup is plain Markdown files and their pictures, not encrypted, so anyone who can read that repository can read those notes: keep it private. Recordings and your teams' notes are left out unless you switch each on.
  • Figma: with your own Figma token, a linked Figma frame is shown with its name and picture, asked of Figma by its link.
  • Calendar: shows your events beside your notes. A calendar address (iCal) you add is fetched by your device. Signing in to Google Calendar passes through our server in the same way as Notion's, and after that your device talks to Google directly. If you turn on adding dated to-dos to your calendar (off unless you do), each open to-do with a date is written to your Google Calendar as an event with the to-do's words.
  • Team chat (Slack and Discord): with a channel's webhook address, your device posts what you send there: a note, and, if you turn them on, a meeting's title, time, action items, decisions, one-sentence summary and an encrypted share link, or your organization's news (who joined or left). With the Ghost bot added to a Discord server and your account linked (we keep your Discord user id and name), /ghost join has the bot record that voice channel, and it says so in the channel. The recording is encrypted to your account's key as it reaches our server, which keeps only that encrypted copy; while the call runs, its title, channel and times are kept in the clear.
  • Meetings (Google Meet and Zoom): when you invite Ghost to a meeting, a bot on our server joins it, says in the meeting's chat that it is there, and records the sound and captions. They are encrypted to your account's key as they reach our server and deleted once one of your devices has collected them; the write-up is made on your device, or by your AI provider. Our server sees the meeting's link, title and times, which it blanks an hour after the meeting ends, and deletes the booking after 30 days. An invitation's organizer and guest list are read and not kept.
  • Email to a note: if you turn it on, you get an address at in.ghostmarkdown.com. Mail sent to it reaches our server through our email provider, Mailgun. Like any mail server, ours receives the mail as it was sent; at once it encrypts it to your account's key and keeps only the encrypted copy, which waits until one of your devices collects it, files it as a note and deletes it. The unencrypted mail is never written to disk or logged. Mailgun handles the mail under its own terms.
  • Publishing and forms: a note or notebook you publish as a site is encrypted like a shared link, with the key in the link after the "#", so we can't read it. A form you share takes answers from anyone with its link; each answer is encrypted in the visitor's browser to your account's key (or your organization members'), so it reaches our server already encrypted and waits there until one of your devices files it.
  • Importing from Todoist: the token you paste is used for that import, sent only to Todoist, and not kept.

What you send to those services is covered by their own privacy policies.

Links, updates and downloads

  • Link previews: to show a preview of a link in a note, your device fetches that page directly. The site sees your IP address, as it would if you opened the link. A YouTube, Spotify or Figma link on a line of its own is shown as a card, with its title and picture asked of that service by its link; its player loads only when you tap it. You can turn previews off in Settings › Account › Privacy.
  • Where a note was written: a new note you make starts with where you are, once you allow Ghost.md to know it, and you can add or remove a note's location from its More sheet. Finding where you are asks your device's location service, which on Android and in a browser uses nearby Wi-Fi and mobile networks and so the service's own servers (Google's, Apple's or your browser's). Turn "Tag new notes with my location" off in Settings › Account › Location on each device to stop it; Local only stops it too. Notes you already have are never tagged.
  • Maps and place names: a note tagged with a location shows a small map, drawn with tiles your device fetches from openstreetmap.org when you open the note, and asks OpenStreetMap's Nominatim once, when the location is written into the note, for the place's name, which is then kept in the note. A new note that never gets a word asks neither. Those servers see your IP address and the coordinates asked about (to about 100 m for the name). The map's tiles show OpenStreetMap's tile servers the rough area being shown, the squares of map around the place rather than the point itself. A place you search for by name, in Remind me at a place, is looked up on Nominatim too. Both are off under Local only, and each has its own switch in Settings › Account › Location. A note's location is part of the note: it syncs with it, end-to-end encrypted like the rest of the note, it is in the note's file in the phone's Files app, and a connected AI assistant sees it. We never see where you are. A shared link leaves it out unless you tick "Share where it was written" on that note.
  • Blanks the AI fills: a question you write in curly brackets, like {?weather tomorrow}, is answered on your device. When it needs something live, such as the weather, an exchange rate or a recent result, and you press Fill, your device asks one public source directly: Open-Meteo for the weather (the place's name, then its coordinates), Frankfurter for the European Central Bank's exchange rates (the two currencies), or Wikipedia and Wikidata (the words of the question in the braces, or for an empty {?} the words of its own sentence that ask). No account or key is used, and nothing else from the note is sent. The source sees your IP address and what was asked. Your device then writes the answer from what came back: the weather and a rate from the source's own figures, and a Wikipedia answer only if its words are in what was returned. Turn "Look up blanks online" off in Settings › Account › Privacy to stop it. Local only stops it too.
  • Place reminders: a to-do can remind you when your phone arrives at a place. Where your phone is stays on it. In the Android app from ghostmarkdown.com, Google Play services' geofences notice the arrival with the app closed, which needs your location all the time. In the Google Play version, place reminders work only while the app is open, and the phone's location is checked about once a minute while it is.
  • Google Play services on Android: scanning a document and reading the words in pictures and PDFs use Google's tools from Google Play services, which run on the phone (Google may receive anonymous diagnostics about them, under its terms), and place reminders use its location service. A Wear OS watch app talks to your phone through Google's Wear OS connection.
  • Updates: the Google Play version is updated by Google Play, and the App Store versions by the App Store; they never check with us. The Android app downloaded from ghostmarkdown.com checks attack.fm, our update server, for a new version. These are plain requests: no account, device id or anything about you is sent. The server sees your IP address.
  • Models: the apps download the voice and formatting models, and the AI models that run on your device if you choose one, from attack.fm, or from Hugging Face if attack.fm can't be reached. These are plain downloads too.

The web version

In a web browser, Ghost.md uses the browser's own speech recognition to turn speech into text. In Chrome, that sends your voice to Google to be transcribed, under Google's terms. The Android, iPhone and Mac apps transcribe on your device instead.

What we don't do

Apart from the anonymous usage statistics above, which you can turn off, we don't use analytics or crash reporting, and we don't use advertising or tracking of any kind: there's no advertising id, and nothing is linked to you across other companies' apps or sites. We don't sell your data or use it to train AI models, and we don't share it with anyone beyond what this page describes: the AI provider and the services you connect yourself, and the location, map and place-name requests, which you can switch off.

Deleting your data

To delete your account, open Ghost.md, go to Settings › Account › Delete account, and enter your password. This deletes, at once and for good, your account and everything it keeps on our servers: your synced notes, recordings, pictures and settings, your shared links, your devices and your recovery codes, your notifications, and your place in every organization (the organizations you had joined are told you left). While you own an organization that anyone else is in, the app asks you to hand it over or delete it first; an organization with nobody else in it is deleted with your account. Your other devices are signed out. The notes on your device stay, because they're your files. More on deleting your account.

Without an account, nothing of yours is on our servers. Uninstalling the app removes its files from your device.

Children

Ghost.md isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.
